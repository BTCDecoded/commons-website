A stated consequence
The contributing guide names repository maintainers as the people who merge. The review practice states no sanction, so that practice is a norm. The guide does not say who receives keys.

Who lands the work

All-time, the top three hold 76.4% of merges, which is a different window. The small set that merges is the guild. The review norm did not disperse it.

Who may change the rule

Who receives keys
The contributing guide governs how a key holder judges consensus. It does not say who receives keys. The 2022 merge concentration is the record of who already has them.

Written clocks

What reaches an outsider
First pull requests by non-maintainers have landed at 16.1% since 2022. Among closed outsider pull requests of 2,000 lines or more, 5.0% landed since 2022. Non-maintainers with five or more prior merges have landed at 68.6% in that window. On a merge outcome that includes self-merges, the maintainer odds ratio is 3.63 before prior merges are controlled and 1.36 after. The gate those findings support is at entry and on large new work. The comparison classes that newcomer pattern as ambient, including secp256k1, BDK, LND, and Eclair. Zero-review self-merges fell from 42.2% in 2017 to 4.0% in the 2026 partial year. Maintainer-authored pull requests that someone else merged with no review fell from 32.4% in 2017 to 8.8% in 2026. That fall is among work that reached review. It is not the filter before review. On the 2026-09-10 roster snapshot, 45.1% of maintainer self-merges have no review object, 1,129 of 2,501. That roster share is not the 2026 yearly rate.

Forms

These cards are the codebook from Bitcoin Has Governance. Each score is a judgment of a form, not a measurement of a tree. The order is the article's. A higher total is not a rank of the implementations above.

Capture resistance
The smallest set of actors whose assent changes the rules, and how concentrated merges are.
Exit cost
The share of economic weight that followed a fork. A higher score means leaving is cheaper.
Legibility
A named decider, and a written rule for who decides. Neither part scores 1. One part scores 2 or 3. Both score 4 or 5.
Error correction
Time from a bad decision to a rollback, and who had to agree.
Credible commitment
How many veto players there are, and how far apart they sit.
Decision cost
Time from proposal to decision. A higher score means the decision is cheaper.
The article's nine forms, in the article's order. Total of 30.
FormTotalConfidenceThe article's reading
Direct democracy——Cannot apply. Participants are pseudonymous, and identities are free to create, so votes cannot be counted.
Token-weighted voting——Cannot apply. Bitcoin has no governance token.
Single maintainer15 of 30lowCan apply, at a legitimacy cost. One obvious author and no written rule.
Foundation steward19 of 30lowCan apply, with legal exposure. A legal entity is one place a suit or a rule can land.
Informal oligarchy15 of 30mediumThe current repository form in the article. A few people merge, chosen by pull request and a meeting, with no written rule for keys or review.
Formal oligarchy18 of 30lowThe same small set, with published rules for selection, review, and removal.
Elected council19 of 30mediumCan apply, with limits. Bitcoin has no agreed electorate.
Mixed constitution21 of 30lowSeparate powers and written thresholds. Untested at Bitcoin's scale. The commons layer table is a written version of this shape. This page does not score that projection.
Ostrom commons22 of 30lowBounded membership, monitoring, and a cheap way to dispute. The cases behind the principles were small and local.
