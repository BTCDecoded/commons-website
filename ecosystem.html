Forms

These cards are the codebook from Bitcoin Has Governance. Each score is a judgment of a form, not a measurement of a tree. The order is the article's. A higher total is not a rank of the implementations above.

Capture resistance
The smallest set of actors whose assent changes the rules, and how concentrated merges are.
Exit cost
The share of economic weight that followed a fork. A higher score means leaving is cheaper.
Legibility
A named decider, and a written rule for who decides. Neither part scores 1. One part scores 2 or 3. Both score 4 or 5.
Error correction
Time from a bad decision to a rollback, and who had to agree.
Credible commitment
How many veto players there are, and how far apart they sit.
Decision cost
Time from proposal to decision. A higher score means the decision is cheaper.

Direct democracy

Cannot apply. Participants are pseudonymous, and identities are free to create, so votes cannot be counted.

Token-weighted voting

Cannot apply. Bitcoin has no governance token.

Single maintainer

Can apply, at a legitimacy cost. One obvious author and no written rule. 15 of 30, low confidence.

Foundation steward

Can apply, with legal exposure. A legal entity is one place a suit or a rule can land. 19 of 30, low confidence.

Informal oligarchy

The current repository form in the article. A few people merge, chosen by pull request and a meeting, with no written rule for keys or review. 15 of 30, medium confidence.

Formal oligarchy

The same small set, with published rules for selection, review, and removal. 18 of 30, low confidence.

Elected council

Can apply, with limits. Bitcoin has no agreed electorate. 19 of 30, medium confidence.

Mixed constitution

Separate powers and written thresholds. 21 of 30, low confidence. Untested at Bitcoin's scale. The commons layer table is a written version of this shape. This page does not score that projection.

Ostrom commons

Bounded membership, monitoring, and a cheap way to dispute. 22 of 30, low confidence. The cases behind the principles were small and local.

Where informal and formal oligarchy differ

Score from 1, worst, to 5, best. Higher is better. For exit cost and decision cost, higher means cheaper. Source: the article's comparison of these two forms. The other forms have a total and no published six-cell row.
